Algebra

Equations & Inequalities

Covers solving simultaneous equations (one linear, one quadratic) by substitution, and using the discriminant to determine how a line and a curve relate. For sketch-based quadratic inequalities, see the Quadratic Functions page.

Key Formulas

Discriminant of $ax^2+bx+c=0$
$$b^2-4ac$$
Valid when: $a \ne 0$.
Line & curve — condition to intersect
$$b^2-4ac \gt 0 \implies \text{2 distinct intersection points}$$
Substitute the line into the curve's equation first to get this quadratic.
Line & curve — condition to be a tangent
$$b^2-4ac = 0 \implies \text{line is a tangent (1 point)}$$
The line touches the curve but does not cross it.
Line & curve — condition not to meet
$$b^2-4ac \lt 0 \implies \text{no intersection}$$
The resulting quadratic has no real roots.

Notes

Solving simultaneous equations (one linear, one quadratic)

  1. Make $x$ or $y$ the subject of the linear equation.
  2. Substitute it into the quadratic equation.
  3. Simplify into the form $ax^2+bx+c=0$ and solve for the first variable.
  4. Substitute each value back into the linear equation to find the matching second variable.
Exam tip: Always substitute into the linear equation (not the quadratic one) to find the second variable — it's faster and avoids picking up extra, invalid roots.
Worked example

Solve $y = x + 1$ and $y = x^2 - x - 1$ simultaneously.

Step 1 — substitute: $x + 1 = x^2 - x - 1$

Step 2 — rearrange: $x^2 - 2x - 2 = 0$

Step 3 — solve (quadratic formula): $$x = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4+8}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}$$

Step 4 — back into $y=x+1$: $$y = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$$

Line and curve: intersecting, tangent, or missing

Substituting a line's equation into a curve's equation reduces the system to a single quadratic. The discriminant of that quadratic then tells you how the line and curve relate — this is the same discriminant test as for the number of roots, just applied after the substitution step.

DiscriminantMeaning
$b^2-4ac \gt 0$Line crosses the curve at 2 distinct points
$b^2-4ac = 0$Line is a tangent to the curve (1 point)
$b^2-4ac \lt 0$Line does not meet the curve
Worked example

Find the value(s) of $k$ for which the line $y = 2x + k$ is a tangent to the curve $y = x^2 + 3x + 5$.

Step 1 — substitute: $2x + k = x^2 + 3x + 5$

Step 2 — rearrange: $x^2 + x + (5-k) = 0$

Step 3 — tangent means equal roots, so discriminant $=0$: $$1^2 - 4(1)(5-k) = 0$$

Step 4 — solve: $$1 - 20 + 4k = 0 \implies k = \frac{19}{4}$$

Exam tip: Watch out for which variable you eliminate. If the linear equation is given as $x = \ldots$, substitute for $x$; don't force it into $y = \ldots$ form first if that introduces fractions you don't need.
