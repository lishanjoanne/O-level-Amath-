Geometry and Trigonometry

Circles

Further coordinate geometry: the equation of a circle in standard and general form. Builds on Coordinate Geometry — distance and midpoint come up constantly here.

Key Formulas

Equation of a circle (standard form)
$$(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2 = r^2$$
Centre $C(a,b)$, radius $r$. From Pythagoras on right triangle $CPQ$: $CQ^2+QP^2=CP^2$.
Equation of a circle (general form)
$$x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c = 0$$
Centre $(-g,-f)$, radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$.

Notes

Standard form and general form are equivalent

Expanding the standard form gives the general form:

Derivation

$$\begin{aligned} (x-a)^2+(y-b)^2 &= r^2 \\ x^2-2ax+a^2+y^2-2by+b^2 &= r^2 \\ x^2+y^2-2ax-2by+(a^2+b^2-r^2) &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

Matching this to $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$ gives $g=-a$, $f=-b$, $c=a^2+b^2-r^2$ — which is exactly where "centre $(-g,-f)$" and "radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$" come from.

Converting general form → standard form

  • Group the $x$ terms and $y$ terms, then complete the square on each: $x^2+2gx = (x+g)^2-g^2$ and $y^2+2fy=(y+f)^2-f^2$.
  • This directly reveals the centre $(-g,-f)$ and radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$ without needing to memorise the formula.
Exam tip: For the general form to represent a real circle, you need $g^2+f^2-c > 0$ (a positive value under the square root). If $g^2+f^2-c=0$ the "circle" is just a single point, and if it's negative there's no real circle at all.

Common exam pattern: circle from a diameter

If $AB$ is a diameter of the circle, use Coordinate Geometry tools directly:

  • Centre = midpoint of $AB$.
  • Radius = half the length of $AB$.
  • Alternatively: any point $P$ on the circle satisfies $\angle APB = 90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle), so $m_{AP} \times m_{BP} = -1$.
