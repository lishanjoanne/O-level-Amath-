Calculus

Kinematics

Displacement, velocity and acceleration linked by differentiation & integration — direction of motion, rest, max/min displacement & velocity, and distance vs. displacement.

Key Formulas

Velocity
$$v = \frac{ds}{dt}$$
Rate of change of displacement $s$ with respect to time $t$.
Acceleration
$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$$
Rate of change of velocity — equivalently, the 2nd derivative of $s$.
Velocity from acceleration
$$v = \int a \, dt$$
Use a given initial condition (e.g. $v$ at $t=0$) to find the constant of integration.
Displacement from velocity
$$s = \int v \, dt$$
Use a given initial condition (e.g. $s$ at $t=0$) to find the constant of integration.

Notes

The differentiate / integrate chain

Differentiate: $s \rightarrow v \rightarrow a$   (each arrow is $\frac{d}{dt}$)

Integrate: $a \rightarrow v \rightarrow s$   (each arrow is $\int \cdot \, dt$)

Every integration introduces a constant — always use the given initial condition to pin it down.

Direction of motion and rest

  • Particle is at rest when $v=0$.
  • Particle moves in the positive direction when $v \gt 0$, and the negative direction when $v \lt 0$.
  • Particle's speed is increasing when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign, and decreasing when they have opposite signs.

Maximum / minimum displacement and velocity

  • Max/min displacement: occurs when $v=0$. Check $a$ at that instant — $a \lt 0$ gives max $s$, $a \gt 0$ gives min $s$.
  • Max/min velocity: occurs when $a=0$. Check $\dfrac{da}{dt}$ at that instant — negative gives max $v$, positive gives min $v$.
Worked example

A particle moves such that $s = t^3-6t^2+9t$ for $t \ge 0$. Find when it is at rest, and determine if each position is a maximum or minimum displacement.

$$v = \frac{ds}{dt} = 3t^2-12t+9 = 3(t-1)(t-3)$$ $$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6t-12$$

At rest: $v=0 \implies t=1$ or $t=3$.

At $t=1$: $a = 6(1)-12=-6 \lt 0$ → maximum displacement, $s=1-6+9=4$.

At $t=3$: $a = 6(3)-12=6 \gt 0$ → minimum displacement, $s=27-54+27=0$.

Distance travelled vs. displacement

Displacement is the net change in position; distance travelled adds up every bit of movement, regardless of direction. They differ whenever the particle reverses direction (i.e. $v=0$) during the time interval.

Method: find every time the particle is at rest within the interval, split the interval at those points, compute $s$ at the start and end of each piece, then sum up $|\Delta s|$ for each piece.
Worked example

Using $s = t^3-6t^2+9t$ from the previous example, find the total distance travelled from $t=0$ to $t=4$.

Rest points inside $[0,4]$: $t=1$ and $t=3$ (found above).

$$s(0)=0 \qquad s(1)=4$$ $$s(3)=0 \qquad s(4)=64-96+36=4$$

Distance travelled:

$$|4-0| + |0-4| + |4-0| = 4+4+4 = 12$$

Compare to displacement, which is just $s(4)-s(0) = 4$.

Exam tip: If the question asks for "distance travelled", always check for rest points inside the interval first — using $|s(\text{end})-s(\text{start})|$ directly only works if the particle never changes direction.
