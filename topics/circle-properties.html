Geometry and Trigonometry

Circle Properties

A-Math's Plane Geometry topic leans on E-Math circle theorems, so both are collected here: all 8 E-Math circle properties, plus the 2 that A-Math actually tests (tangent–chord theorem and the midpoint theorem). Originally built as its own site at circleproperties.vercel.app.

E-Math — Geometrical Properties of Circles

Symmetric Property 1

Perpendicular bisector of a chord

A line from the centre, drawn perpendicular to a chord, bisects that chord — and the converse holds too: the perpendicular bisector of any chord always passes through the centre.

(⊥ bisector of chord)
Symmetric Property 2

Equal chords, equal distance

Equal chords sit the same distance from the centre; chords equidistant from the centre are equal in length. Works both ways — length tells you distance, distance tells you length.

(equal chords, equidistant from centre)
Symmetric Property 3

Tangent perpendicular to radius

A tangent meets the circle at exactly one point, and at that point of contact it is always perpendicular to the radius.

(tan ⊥ rad)
Symmetric Property 4

Tangents from an external point

The two tangents drawn to a circle from any point outside it are always equal in length — and OP bisects the angle between them.

(tangents from ext. pt.)
Angle Property 1

Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference

An angle formed at the centre by a chord is always exactly double the angle the same chord forms at the circumference, on the major arc.

(∠ at centre = 2∠ at circumference)
Angle Property 2

Right angle in a semicircle

When a chord is a diameter, any angle it subtends at the circumference — from either arc — is exactly 90°.

(rt. ∠ in semicircle)
Angle Property 3

Angles in the same segment

Any two angles subtended by the same chord, from points on the same arc, are equal — P and Q can slide anywhere along that arc and the angle stays the same.

(∠s in same segment)
Angle Property 4

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral

In a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle, opposite angles always add up to 180° — a + c = 180° and b + d = 180°.

(∠s in opp. segments are supp.)

A-Math — Plane Geometry

Tangent–Chord Theorem

Alternate Segment Theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord, taken at the point of contact, equals the angle the chord subtends in the alternate segment: ∠APB = ∠BAT.

(tan-chord ∠)
Plane Geometry — Triangles

Midpoint Theorem

Not a circle property — included here because it turns up alongside the tangent-chord theorem in Plane Geometry questions. If D and E are the midpoints of AB and AC, then DE is parallel to BC, and DE = ½BC.

(mid-pt theorem)

Notes

Why only two circle properties show up in A-Math

The rest of the circle theorems (perpendicular bisector, equal chords, angle at centre, angles in the same segment, cyclic quadrilaterals...) belong to the E-Math syllabus. A-Math's Plane Geometry topic only draws on the tangent–chord theorem, paired here with the midpoint theorem since both get tested in the same question type.

Exam tip: Even though only the tangent–chord theorem is examinable directly in A-Math, Plane Geometry questions often route through an E-Math circle property first (e.g. spotting a right angle in a semicircle, or an isosceles triangle from two radii) before the tangent–chord step becomes visible. Know all 8 E-Math properties cold.
