Geometry and Trigonometry

Coordinate Geometry

Length & midpoint of a line segment, gradients, parallel/perpendicular lines, the equation of a straight line, and the area of a rectilinear figure. See also Circles for the coordinate geometry of circles.

Key Formulas

Length of a line segment
$$AB = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$$
Distance between $A(x_1,y_1)$ and $B(x_2,y_2)$, from Pythagoras' theorem.
Midpoint of a line segment
$$\text{Midpoint of } AB = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$
The average of the $x$-coordinates, and the average of the $y$-coordinates.
Gradient of a line
$$m_{AB} = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \tan\theta$$
$\theta$ is the angle the line makes with the positive $x$-axis direction.
Horizontal & vertical lines
$$\text{gradient of horizontal line} = 0 \qquad \text{gradient of vertical line} = \text{undefined}$$
Parallel lines
$$l_1 \parallel l_2 \iff m_1 = m_2$$
Perpendicular lines
$$l_1 \perp l_2 \iff m_1 m_2 = -1$$
Provided neither line is vertical.
Collinear points
$$\text{gradient of } AB = \text{gradient of } BC = \text{gradient of } AC$$
$A$, $B$, $C$ lie on the same straight line.
Perpendicular bisector
$$\text{gradient} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}}, \text{ passing through the midpoint of } AB$$
The line perpendicular to $AB$ that passes through $AB$'s midpoint.
Equation of horizontal / vertical lines
$$y = b \qquad x = a$$
Equation of a non-vertical line
$$y = mx+c \qquad \text{or} \qquad y-y_1 = m(x-x_1)$$
$c$ is the $y$-intercept; $(x_1,y_1)$ is any known point on the line.
Area of a triangle
$$\text{Area of } \triangle ABC = \frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix} x_1 & x_2 & x_3 & x_1 \\ y_1 & y_2 & y_3 & y_1 \end{vmatrix}$$
"Shoelace" pattern: sum the down-diagonal products, subtract the up-diagonal products, take half the absolute value. Vertices listed anticlockwise.
Area of an n-sided polygon
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix} x_1 & x_2 & \cdots & x_n & x_1 \\ y_1 & y_2 & \cdots & y_n & y_1 \end{vmatrix}$$
Same shoelace pattern extended to $n$ vertices, listed in order (anticlockwise) around the polygon.

Notes

Finding the equation of a non-vertical line

  1. Find the gradient, $m$, of the line.
  2. Identify the coordinates of a point, $(x_1,y_1)$, that lies on the line.
  3. Substitute $m$, $x_1$ and $y_1$ into $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ — or into $y=mx+c$ to solve for $c$ (the $y$-intercept).

Working the shoelace formula

Write the $x$-coordinates on top and $y$-coordinates below, repeating the first column at the end. Multiply diagonally down-right and add them up; multiply diagonally down-left and add them up; subtract the second sum from the first, then take half the absolute value.

Worked example

Find the area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,2)$, $B(4,6)$, $C(6,1)$.

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\left|\,(1\times6 + 4\times1 + 6\times2) - (2\times4 + 6\times6 + 1\times1)\,\right|$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}\left|\,(6+4+12) - (8+36+1)\,\right| = \frac{1}{2}|22-45| = \frac{23}{2} = 11.5 \text{ units}^2$$

Exam tip: If your shoelace calculation gives a negative value before taking the absolute value, the vertices were listed clockwise instead of anticlockwise — the area itself is still correct once you take $|\,\cdot\,|$.
