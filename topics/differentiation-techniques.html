Calculus

Differentiation Techniques

Covers all standard syllabus formulas plus the rule-by-rule "steps" from your own notes. Send over worked examples or common student mistakes and they'll be added in.

Key Formulas

Power rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$$
Sum / difference rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}\big(f(x) \pm g(x)\big) = f'(x) \pm g'(x)$$
Chain rule
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$$
Used for composite functions, e.g. $y = (3x+1)^5$.
Chain rule — power form
$$\frac{d}{dx}\big[f(x)\big]^n = n\big[f(x)\big]^{n-1} \cdot f'(x)$$
Bring down the power → reduce the power by 1 → multiply by the derivative of "the inside".
Product rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx}$$
Quotient rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) = \frac{v\frac{du}{dx} - u\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$$
Exponential functions
$$\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(e^{f(x)}) = f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$$
Logarithmic functions
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x} \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\big(\ln f(x)\big) = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$$
Trigonometric functions
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(\tan x) = \sec^2 x$$
$x$ measured in radians.
Trig functions with chain rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}\sin f(x) = f'(x)\cos f(x) \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos f(x) = -f'(x)\sin f(x) \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\tan f(x) = f'(x)\sec^2 f(x)$$
Trig functions with linear argument
$$\frac{d}{dx}\sin(ax+b) = a\cos(ax+b) \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos(ax+b) = -a\sin(ax+b) \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\tan(ax+b) = a\sec^2(ax+b)$$
Powers of trig functions
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin^n x) = n\sin^{n-1}x\cos x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(\cos^n x) = -n\cos^{n-1}x\sin x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(\tan^n x) = n\tan^{n-1}x\sec^2 x$$
Chain rule form: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\big[\sin^n f(x)\big] = n\sin^{n-1}f(x) \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}\big[\sin f(x)\big]$, and similarly for $\cos^n f(x)$, $\tan^n f(x)$.

Notes

Choosing the right rule

  • One function "inside" another (e.g. $(2x+3)^4$, $\sin(3x)$, $e^{5x}$) → chain rule.
  • Two functions multiplied together (e.g. $x^2 \sin x$) → product rule.
  • One function divided by another (e.g. $\frac{x}{\cos x}$) → quotient rule.
  • Rules often combine — e.g. product rule where one factor itself needs the chain rule.

Remembering the rules, step by step

  • Chain rule (power form): Step 1 — bring down the power. Step 2 — reduce the power by 1. Step 3 — differentiate "the inside".
  • Product rule: Step 1 — copy the first term, multiply by the derivative of the second. Step 2 — copy the second term, multiply by the derivative of the first. Step 3 — add the two.
  • Quotient rule: Step 1 — square the denominator. Step 2 — copy the denominator, multiply by the derivative of the numerator. Step 3 — copy the numerator, multiply by the derivative of the denominator. Step 4 — subtract Step 3's term from Step 2's to form the numerator.

Applications

  • Tangents & normals: gradient of tangent $= \frac{dy}{dx}$ at that point; gradient of normal $= -1 \div \frac{dy}{dx}$.
  • Stationary points: solve $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$; use the second derivative or a sign table to classify as max/min.
  • Rates of change: connect two rates with the chain rule, e.g. $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dr} \cdot \frac{dr}{dt}$.
Exam tip: Always simplify/factorise $\frac{dy}{dx}$ before solving for stationary points — an unfactorised expression makes it easy to lose solutions.
