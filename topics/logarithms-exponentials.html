Logarithms & Exponentials

Covers all standard syllabus formulas plus the worked example, common mistakes and exam tips from your own sketch notes. Send over more and they'll be added in.

Key Formulas

Definition of a logarithm
$$a^x = N \iff \log_a N = x$$
Valid when: $a > 0$, $a \ne 1$, $N > 0$.
Product law
$$\log_a(xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$$
Quotient law
$$\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a x - \log_a y$$
Power law
$$\log_a(x^n) = n\log_a x$$
Change of base
$$\log_a x = \frac{\log_b x}{\log_b a}$$
Used to switch to base 10 or base $e$ for calculator work.
Special values
$$\log_a 1 = 0 \qquad \log_a a = 1 \qquad a^{\log_a x} = x$$
Natural log & exponential
$$e^x = y \iff \ln y = x \qquad \ln e = 1 \qquad \ln 1 = 0$$
Laws of indices
$$a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$
Negative & fractional indices
$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$$
Zero exponent & power of a product/quotient
$$a^0 = 1 \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n \qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}$$
Valid when: $a \ne 0$ (for $a^0$), $b \ne 0$ (for the quotient).

Notes

Solving logarithmic equations

  • Combine log terms on each side into a single log using the product/quotient/power laws first.
  • Once both sides are single logs to the same base, drop the logs and equate the arguments.
  • Always check solutions against the domain ($N > 0$) — reject any answer that makes an argument negative or zero.

Solving exponential equations

  • If both sides can be written as powers of the same base, equate the indices directly.
  • Otherwise, take $\lg$ or $\ln$ of both sides and use the power law to bring the index down.
  • Watch for equations that are quadratic in disguise, e.g. $e^{2x} - 3e^x + 2 = 0$ — substitute $u = e^x$ first.
Worked example
  1. Make the bases the same, if possible.
  2. If not, take logs of both sides.
  3. Equate/expand the powers.
  4. Solve for the unknown.

Solve $2^{x+1} = 5^{x-1}$.

Take $\lg$ of both sides: $(x+1)\lg 2 = (x-1)\lg 5$

$x\lg 2 + \lg 2 = x\lg 5 - \lg 5$

$x(\lg 2 - \lg 5) = -\lg 5 - \lg 2$

$$x = \frac{-\lg 5 - \lg 2}{\lg 2 - \lg 5}$$

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the conditions: $a > 0$, $a \ne 1$, $y > 0$.
  • $\log_a(M + N) \ne \log_a M + \log_a N$.
  • $\log_a(M^n) \ne (\log_a M)^n$.
  • Dropping the base $a$ when writing logs.
  • Forgetting to check for extraneous solutions.

Exam tips

  • Write out the conditions ($a>0$, $a \ne 1$, $y>0$) when a question requires them.
  • Use the "make $x$ the subject" strategy for equations.
  • If unsure how to start, take logs of both sides.
  • Give exact answers in terms of logs unless told to round.
  • Use the GC wisely, but show working — method marks need the steps, not just the final answer.
Exam tip: $\log_a(x + y) \ne \log_a x + \log_a y$. The product law only applies to $\log_a x + \log_a y = \log_a(xy)$ — a very common careless error.
