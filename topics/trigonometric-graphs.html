Geometry and Trigonometry

Trigonometric Graphs

The graphs of $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\tan x$, plus the scaled/stretched family $a\sin bx$, $a\cos bx$, $a\tan bx$. See also Trigonometric Ratios.

Key Formulas — $y=\sin x$, $y=\cos x$, $y=\tan x$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$

$y = \sin x$
$\sin x=0$ at $x=0^\circ,180^\circ,360^\circ$; $\sin x=1$ at $x=90^\circ$; $\sin x=-1$ at $x=270^\circ$.
Period $=360^\circ$, amplitude $=1$, asymptotes: none.
$y = \cos x$
$\cos x=0$ at $x=90^\circ,270^\circ$; $\cos x=1$ at $x=0^\circ,360^\circ$; $\cos x=-1$ at $x=180^\circ$.
Period $=360^\circ$, amplitude $=1$, asymptotes: none.
$y = \tan x$
$\tan x=0$ at $x=0^\circ,180^\circ,360^\circ$.
Period $=180^\circ$, amplitude: undefined, asymptotes: $x=90^\circ, x=270^\circ$.

Key Formulas — $y=a\sin bx$, $y=a\cos bx$, $y=a\tan bx$ ($a>0$)

$y = a\sin bx$
$$\text{Range: } -a \le a\sin bx \le a$$
Period $=\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}$, amplitude $=|a|$, asymptotes: none.
$y = a\cos bx$
$$\text{Range: } -a \le a\cos bx \le a$$
Period $=\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}$, amplitude $=|a|$, asymptotes: none. Same shape as $a\sin bx$, shifted to start at a peak.
$y = a\tan bx$
$$\text{Range: any real number}$$
Period $=\dfrac{180^\circ}{b}$, amplitude: undefined, asymptotes: $x=\dfrac{90^\circ}{b}, x=\dfrac{270^\circ}{b}$.

Notes

Reading off period, amplitude and asymptotes

  • $a$ stretches the graph vertically — it scales the amplitude, but never shifts the graph sideways.
  • $b$ compresses the graph horizontally — a bigger $b$ means a shorter period (more cycles packed into the same width).
  • For $\tan$, the asymptotes scale the same way the period does: divide the basic $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ positions by $b$.
