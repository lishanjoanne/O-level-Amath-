Quadratic Functions

Covers completing the square, the nature of roots via the discriminant, and solving quadratic inequalities properly by sketching. Send over more worked examples and they'll be added in.

Key Formulas

Completing the square (monic)
$$x^2 + px = \left(x + \frac{p}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^2$$
Add and subtract $\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^2$ — half the coefficient of $x$, squared.
Completing the square — worked example ($a \ne 1$)
$$\begin{aligned} 2x^2+12x+5 &= 2(x^2+6x)+5 \\ &= 2\big[(x+3)^2-9\big]+5 \\ &= 2(x+3)^2-13 \end{aligned}$$
Factor out the 2 first, then complete the square inside the bracket.
Three equivalent forms
$$\begin{aligned} y &= ax^2+bx+c \\ &= a(x-p)(x-q) \\ &= a(x-h)^2+k \end{aligned}$$
Valid when: $a \ne 0$. Pick the form that gives you what the question asks for.
Turning point (vertex form)
$$y = a(x-h)^2+k$$
Turning point is $(h,k)$: minimum if $a \gt 0$, maximum if $a \lt 0$. Line of symmetry: $x=h$.
x-intercepts (factorised form)
$$y = a(x-p)(x-q)$$
$x$-intercepts are $p$ and $q$. Line of symmetry: $x = \dfrac{p+q}{2}$.
Always positive / always negative
$$\begin{aligned} ax^2+bx+c \gt 0 &\text{ for all } x \\ \iff a \gt 0 &\text{ and } b^2-4ac \lt 0 \end{aligned}$$
Swap both inequality signs (to $a \lt 0$ and stays $b^2-4ac \lt 0$) for "always negative".

Notes

Sketching a quadratic graph $y=ax^2+bx+c$

  1. Determine whether the graph is $\cup$-shaped ($a \gt 0$) or $\cap$-shaped ($a \lt 0$).
  2. Express the function as $y=a(x-p)(x-q)$ to get the $x$-intercepts, or $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ to get the turning point.
  3. Substitute $x=0$ into the equation to find the $y$-intercept.

Nature of roots — the discriminant

For $ax^2+bx+c=0$, the roots are the $x$-coordinates where the curve $y=ax^2+bx+c$ crosses the $x$-axis. Their nature depends on the discriminant $b^2-4ac$:

DiscriminantNature of rootsx-axis intersections
$b^2-4ac \gt 0$2 real and distinct roots2
$b^2-4ac=0$2 real and equal (repeated) roots1 (curve touches the axis)
$b^2-4ac \ge 0$2 real roots1 or 2
$b^2-4ac \lt 0$No real roots0
Same test, another use: a line and a curve are solved as simultaneous equations, which reduces to one quadratic. The same discriminant test then tells you the number of intersection points: $b^2-4ac \gt 0$ → line crosses the curve at 2 points; $=0$ → the line is a tangent (1 point); $\lt 0$ → the line doesn't meet the curve at all.

Solving quadratic inequalities

  1. Make one side of the inequality zero (rearrange everything onto the LHS).
  2. Factorise the quadratic expression.
  3. Sketch the graph and label the $x$-intercepts $p$ and $q$ (where $p \lt q$).
  4. Read the solution off the sketch, based on the inequality sign.
Exam tip: Always make sure the RHS of the inequality is zero before factorising the expression on the LHS — factorising too early against a nonzero RHS is a common careless mistake.
Worked example

Solve $x^2 - x \gt 12$.

Step 1 — make RHS zero: $x^2 - x - 12 \gt 0$

Step 2 — factorise: $(x-4)(x+3) \gt 0$

Step 3 — sketch: a $\cup$-shaped curve ($a=1 \gt 0$) crossing the $x$-axis at $x=-3$ and $x=4$.

Step 4 — read off (want $\gt 0$, i.e. above the axis): $$x \lt -3 \text{ or } x \gt 4$$
